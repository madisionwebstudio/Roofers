Why Google Maps matters

The map is where local calls start.

For most roofing searches, Google shows a map with three businesses before the regular results. Those three spots — the local pack — are decided mainly by relevance, distance and prominence: how well your profile matches the search, how close you are to the homeowner, and how well-known and well-reviewed your company is.

Distance you can't change. Everything else is work we can do: a complete profile, steady genuine reviews, consistent listings and a website that backs it all up.

Google Business Profile

Your profile is your second homepage.

Many homeowners never reach your website. They see your Google Business Profile in Maps, read a few reviews, look at the photos and tap “Call” or “Website” right there. That's why we treat the profile as a page that needs the same care as your homepage.

A complete, accurate profile helps Google understand which searches you're relevant for, and it helps the homeowner decide in seconds. A half-empty one, with stock photos and no replies to reviews, quietly sends them to the next roofer in the list.

  • The right categories“Roofing contractor” as primary, plus only the secondary categories that match work you really do.
  • Services listed one by oneReplacement, repair, storm damage, inspections, gutters — each with a plain description.
  • Real photos, added regularlyYour crew, your trucks and finished roofs in your towns. No stock images.
  • Posts and Q&AShort updates on recent jobs, storm season and offers, and clear answers to common questions.
  • A website link that matchesThe profile points to a page that repeats the same name, services and towns — so Google sees one consistent business.

City pages

What makes a city page actually local.

A city page earns its place when a homeowner in that town reads it and thinks “these people work here.” That takes details only a local roofer would know — not a template with the town name swapped in.

  • Jobs you've done therePhotos and short notes from real projects in that town or nearby neighborhoods.
  • Local weather and roof typesHail, wind, heat or snow — and what that means for the roofs homeowners there have.
  • Permits and HOAsWhat homeowners should expect from the local permit process and common HOA rules — checked, not guessed.
  • Reviews from that areaGenuine reviews from customers in or near that town, shown with their permission.
