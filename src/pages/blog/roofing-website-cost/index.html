The five ways to get a roofing website

1. Build it yourself with a website builder

Typical cost: a monthly subscription, often in the range of roughly $15–$50 a month for business plans [SOURCE NEEDED: check current Wix, Squarespace and GoDaddy plan pricing], plus your time.

Builders like Wix, Squarespace and GoDaddy have improved a lot. If you're just starting out and money is tight, a clean, simple builder site is far better than nothing.

The real cost is time — usually more than people expect — and the result tends to look like what it is: a template. A common story: it was fine to start, but it never ranked, and there was never time to improve it.

2. A freelancer

Typical cost: very wide — from a few hundred dollars on freelance marketplaces to several thousand for an experienced local designer [SOURCE NEEDED: freelance web design rate survey].

A good freelancer can be excellent value. The risks are on the edges: what happens if they disappear, who hosts the site, who updates it, and whether they understand roofing searches or just make it look nice. Ask for examples of contractor sites they've built and how those sites perform.

3. A general web design agency

Typical cost: commonly $3,000–$10,000 upfront for a small-business site, sometimes much more, plus a monthly fee for hosting and maintenance [SOURCE NEEDED: agency pricing survey].

You usually get a proper process, a designer and a developer, and a polished result. The catch for roofers: you're paying for a team that's learning your industry on your project, and you pay most or all of it before you see the finished site.

4. A roofing-specific agency

Typical cost: varies widely. Some charge a large upfront build fee, some a monthly subscription, and many bundle the site with SEO or ads on a long contract.

Specialists know what roofing sites need — storm pages, insurance help, financing, service areas. Read the contract carefully, though. Some monthly plans mean you never own the site; stop paying, and it's gone.

5. A monthly website plan you own after a set term

Typical cost: a flat monthly fee covering design, hosting and care, with ownership after a minimum term. This is how our $199/month plan works: 12-month minimum, then the site is yours.

It spreads the cost out like a lease-to-own. The things to check are the minimum term, what happens if you leave early, and whether you really own the site at the end.

Costs nobody mentions upfront

The build price is rarely the whole bill. Ask about each of these.

  • Domain name. Usually a small yearly fee [SOURCE NEEDED: typical .com renewal price]. It should be registered in your name.
  • Hosting. Some quotes include it, many don't. Cheap hosting can make a site slow.
  • Updates and security. Sites built on WordPress need regular plugin and theme updates. Someone has to do them — and fix what breaks.
  • Edits. Is changing a phone number or adding photos free, or billed by the hour?
  • Copywriting. Some designers expect you to write every page yourself. That's often where projects stall for months.
  • Photos. A professional shoot is extra almost everywhere. Good phone photos of real jobs are often enough.
  • Logo. Usually a separate project.
  • SEO. “SEO-friendly” is not the same as SEO. Getting found for competitive roofing searches is ongoing work, priced separately.

What actually makes a site worth the money

A roofing website is worth whatever jobs it brings in. Since one replacement can be worth thousands to your business, even one extra job a year can pay for a decent site — but only if the site actually produces calls. These are the things that move the needle:

  1. It's fast and easy on a phone. Test any provider's past work in Google PageSpeed Insights before hiring them.
  2. It has separate pages for your services and areas — replacement, repair, storm damage, the towns you serve. One-page sites rarely rank.
  3. It makes calling effortless. Tap-to-call, a short quote form, a sticky call button.
  4. It looks like your company. Real photos, real reviews, real people.
  5. You can change it. A site you can't update goes stale, and stale sites lose trust.

Is a cheap website ever the right call?

Yes — if you're brand new, mostly working referrals, and just need somewhere to send people who want to check you're real. A simple, honest site with your phone number, services, photos and reviews beats an expensive one that never launches.

Where cheap gets expensive is when the site can't grow with you: you can't add pages for new services or towns, it's too slow to rank, or you don't own it. Then you pay twice — once for the cheap site, and again for the rebuild.

Seven questions to ask before you pay anyone

  1. Can I see the design before I pay in full? Many providers ask for 50% or more upfront. Ask what you get to see first.
  2. Who owns the domain, the site and the accounts? Get the answer in writing.
  3. What happens if I stop paying the monthly fee? Do you keep the site, get a copy, or lose everything?
  4. What's the minimum contract? And what does it cost to leave early?
  5. Who writes the content?
  6. How do I make small changes, and what do they cost?
  7. Can I see three roofing or contractor sites you've built? Then check them on your phone.

Quick answers

How long does a roofing website take to build?

Anything from a few days for a simple builder site to a few months for a large agency project. The biggest delay is almost never the design — it's waiting on content: photos, service details and page text. Choose a provider who writes the content for you if your time is short.

Should my website and SEO come from the same company?

It helps, because the person doing SEO can change the site directly instead of filing requests with someone else. But it's not required. What matters is that whoever does SEO has full access to edit pages, titles and content.

Can I keep my current domain?

Almost always, yes — and you should. Your domain carries your history with Google. A good rebuild keeps it, and redirects every old page to its new address so you don't lose rankings you've already earned.

Is WordPress bad for roofers?

No. WordPress runs a huge share of the web and can be excellent. The problems come from heavy themes, too many plugins and nobody keeping them updated. If you go with WordPress, make sure someone is responsible for updates and security every month.
