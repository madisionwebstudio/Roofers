How Google picks the Maps top 3

Google says local results are based mainly on three things: relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance (how far you are from the searcher, or the location in the search) and prominence (how well-known and well-reviewed your business is) [SOURCE NEEDED: Google Business Profile Help — how Google determines local ranking].

Two consequences matter for roofers:

  • There isn't one ranking. Your Maps position changes depending on where the homeowner is standing. You may rank first near your address and nowhere ten miles out. That's why a single “we're #2!” screenshot is misleading.
  • Distance is the one factor you can't optimize. You can't move your business to every suburb. So you win by being clearly the most relevant and most prominent roofer in the area you serve.

Step 1: Get your Google Business Profile right

Your Google Business Profile is the single biggest lever for Maps rankings. Treat it like a second homepage.

Primary category

Choose “Roofing contractor” as your primary category — it decides which searches you're eligible for. Add secondary categories only if they're genuinely true (for example, gutter or siding services).

Business name

Use your real business name, exactly as it appears on your truck and paperwork. Adding keywords (“Best Roofing San Antonio Roof Repair”) is against Google's guidelines and a common reason profiles get suspended [SOURCE NEEDED: Google Business Profile guidelines on business names].

Address vs. service area

If customers don't come to you, hide your address and set a service area of the towns you actually serve. Never use a virtual office or mailbox address to appear local — it risks suspension.

Complete everything

  • Services: list each one (roof replacement, roof repair, storm damage, inspections) with a short description.
  • Description: plain words about what you do and where.
  • Hours: accurate, including holidays.
  • Photos: real job photos, added regularly.
  • Posts and Q&A: a post when you finish a job or a storm hits; answer the real questions homeowners ask.

Step 2: Build a steady stream of reviews

Reviews feed prominence, and they decide whether a homeowner taps your listing or the one above it. What matters isn't just the total:

  • Count compared with competitors — you need to be in the same league as the roofers already in the top 3.
  • Steady pace — a few every month looks real; twenty in one week looks bought.
  • Recency — recent reviews carry more weight with homeowners.
  • Detail — reviews that mention the job and the town (“replaced our roof in Stone Oak after the hail”) help with relevance.
  • Your replies — reply to every review, good and bad, calmly and quickly.

The simplest system: ask in person at the moment the homeowner is happiest — when the job is done and the yard is clean — then text them the direct review link that same day. Follow up once, never more. Never offer anything in exchange, and never ask only happy customers while steering unhappy ones elsewhere (“review gating”) — both break platform rules.

Step 3: Give Google a website worth trusting

Your website supports your Maps rankings. Google reads it to understand what you do and where. A roofing website that helps:

  • Has a separate page for each service — roof replacement, roof repair, storm damage — not one page that lists everything.
  • Has real city pages for your most important towns, with local detail (neighborhoods, common roof types, recent storms) rather than the same text with the city swapped.
  • Shows your name, address (if public) and phone exactly as they appear on your Google profile.
  • Uses LocalBusiness or Organization schema so the facts are machine-readable.
  • Loads fast on a phone and makes calling one tap away — so the visits Maps sends you turn into calls.

Link your Google Business Profile to the most relevant page — usually your homepage, or a city page if you have a profile for that area.

Step 4: Consistent citations

Citations are listings of your business on other sites — Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, the BBB, Facebook, trade directories. They matter less than they used to, but inconsistency still hurts: if your phone number is different on three sites, Google is less sure which is right.

Write down your official name, address and phone once, and copy it exactly everywhere. Twenty accurate, relevant listings beat hundreds from a cheap directory blast.

Step 5: Local links and mentions

Links from real local sources build prominence: a local supplier listing you as a customer, a youth sports team you sponsor, your chamber of commerce, a local news story about storm cleanup. These are cheap, genuine, and hard for competitors to copy. Avoid paid link packages — the risk isn't worth it for a business with a real name.

What a good city page looks like

City pages help you show up for searches that name a town, especially towns a little further from your address. They only work if they're genuinely about that town:

  • The city and state in the title and main heading (“Roof Replacement in Round Rock, TX”).
  • An opening that shows local knowledge — the neighborhoods you work in, common roof types, the storms that hit the area.
  • Photos of jobs you've actually done there.
  • Reviews from customers in that town, if you have them.
  • Local questions answered — permits, HOAs, insurance timing.
  • Your phone number and a quote form.

The test: delete the city name. If the page is now identical to your other city pages, it isn't a city page — it's a copy. Start with your two or three most important towns and do them properly.

Mistakes that hold roofers back in Maps

  • Keywords in the business name. It may work briefly, then a competitor reports it and the profile is suspended — invisible in Maps until it's reinstated.
  • A fake or virtual address in a town you want to rank in. Same risk, and homeowners lose trust when they discover it.
  • Changing name, address, category and phone all at once on an established profile. Big sudden changes can trigger re-verification.
  • Ignoring reviews — no new ones for months, or angry ones left unanswered.
  • Linking the profile to a slow, generic website that doesn't mention the services or towns you want to rank for.
  • Thirty thin city pages with only the town name changed. Google treats near-duplicate city pages as low quality; three genuinely local pages beat thirty copies.

Quick answers

Can I rank in Maps in a town where I don't have an address?

It's hard, because distance is part of the ranking. You can still appear for searches that name the town, and you can rank in the regular results with a strong city page. Never fake an address to get around it.

How many reviews do I need?

There's no magic number. Look at the roofers already in the top 3 for your main search and aim to be in the same range — with a steady pace of new ones.

Do Google Business Profile posts help?

They're a small signal at most, but they show homeowners the business is active, and they're a good place to share recent jobs and storm updates.

Should I pay for a “guaranteed top 3” service?

No. Nobody controls Google's rankings. Pay for specific, visible work and honest reporting instead.

How to measure progress

Because Maps rankings change with location, use a grid rank tracker that checks your position from many points across your service area. Alongside it, watch:

  • Profile actions in your Google Business Profile (calls, website clicks, direction requests).
  • Calls and form leads from Maps, tracked separately from other sources.
  • Review count and rating against the roofers in the top 3.

How long it takes

For most roofing companies, profile and on-page fixes start to show early movement in the first 1–3 months, with stronger results in 3–6 months as reviews and pages build up. Big metros with many established roofers can take longer. Anyone who guarantees you a top-3 spot by a date is guessing.
