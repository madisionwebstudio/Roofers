The short answer

  • Need leads in the next few weeks? Start with Google Ads — if your website is ready to convert.
  • Can wait a few months and want lower cost per lead over time? Start with SEO.
  • Have the budget for both? Run ads now while SEO builds, then shift budget as rankings grow.
  • Website slow or outdated? Fix that first. Both channels send people there.

When Google Ads should come first

  • You need jobs now — new company, slow season, crew sitting idle.
  • A storm just hit your area and homeowners are searching for inspections.
  • You're entering a new town where you have no rankings or reviews yet.
  • You want data fast — which searches and towns bring calls — to guide your SEO later.

Ads work best with tight campaigns (replacement, repair, storm), towns you actually serve, negative keywords that block job seekers and DIY searches, and landing pages that match each ad. Read our Google Ads guide for roofing companies or see roofing PPC management.

When SEO should come first

  • Your budget is tight and ad clicks in your area are expensive.
  • You're already busy and want steady leads 6–12 months from now.
  • Your Google Business Profile is weak — few reviews, few photos, wrong category. That's quick SEO work with a big effect on Maps.
  • You serve a smaller market where a few strong pages and a good profile can win most local searches.

Three example situations

These are illustrations, not client results — they show how the decision usually plays out.

A new two-truck company in a mid-size town

Few reviews, a basic website, cash is tight. The best first move is the free work: Google Business Profile, a review routine, real photos and a fast, simple website. Then a small, tightly targeted ad campaign for replacement and repair in the home town, to bring jobs while the profile grows. SEO work on service pages follows as soon as the budget allows.

An established roofer with a strong Maps listing

Lots of reviews and steady referral work, but the website is old and ranks for little beyond the company name. SEO is the bigger opportunity: service pages, town pages and technical fixes can turn existing reputation into organic leads. Ads become a top-up for storms and slow months.

A roofer expanding into a neighboring metro

No address there, so Maps rankings will be hard at first — distance is part of how Google ranks local results. Ads give instant visibility in the new towns, while genuinely local pages and reviews from jobs there build organic presence over time.

Why most roofers run both

The two channels help each other:

  • Ads show you which searches and towns actually bring jobs — SEO can then target those.
  • A homeowner who sees your ad and your Maps listing with good reviews is more likely to trust you.
  • As SEO rankings grow, you can lower ad spend on searches you already win for free.
  • During storms, ads let you scale up fast without waiting for rankings.

Common mistakes with each

Google Ads mistakes

  • Broad keywords with no negatives — paying for “roofing jobs” and “how to shingle a roof.”
  • Sending every ad to the homepage instead of a matching page.
  • Targeting a whole metro when you only want jobs in a few towns.
  • Not tracking calls and forms as conversions, so Google optimizes for clicks.

SEO mistakes

  • Keywords in the Google Business Profile name — a fast route to suspension.
  • Dozens of thin, copy-paste city pages.
  • Buying links or reviews.
  • Giving up after two months, before the work has time to show.

Splitting a budget

There's no universal split. A practical way to start:

  1. Make sure the website and Google Business Profile are solid — that's the foundation for both.
  2. Set an ad budget you can keep for at least 2–3 months so the data means something.
  3. Run SEO at the same time if you can — month to month, so you're not locked in.
  4. Track every lead to its source and compare cost per signed job monthly.
  5. Move money toward whichever channel signs jobs cheaper in your market.

Quick answers

Is SEO or Google Ads better for roofers?

Neither is better in every case. Ads are faster and more controllable; SEO is slower but keeps working without a cost per click. Most roofers do best running both.

How long does roofing SEO take?

Early movement often shows in one to three months, with stronger results in three to six months. Competitive metros can take longer.

How much should I spend on Google Ads as a roofer?

Enough to run for at least two to three months in the towns and jobs you care about. We require a $2,000/month minimum ad budget paid to Google so campaigns get enough data to improve.

Can I stop Google Ads once SEO is working?

You can reduce spend on searches you already rank well for. Many roofers keep a smaller ad budget for storms, new towns and high-value jobs.
