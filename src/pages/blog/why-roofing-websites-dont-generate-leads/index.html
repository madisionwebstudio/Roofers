First: is it traffic or conversion?

A website that doesn't generate leads has one of two problems: not enough of the right people visit (traffic), or visitors don't contact you (conversion). The fixes are very different.

Open Google Analytics (or your website builder's stats) and Google Search Console. If you get very few visits from your towns, start with SEO and your Google profile. If you get a fair number of visits but almost no form fills or messages, the website itself is losing them — that's what this article is about.

No analytics installed? That's problem number zero. You can't fix what you can't see.

The 10 most common problems

1. It's slow on phones

Heavy images, sliders, video backgrounds and dozens of plugins can make a roofing site take several seconds to show anything on mobile. Many people leave before it loads. Fix: compress images, remove sliders and unused scripts, and test with Google PageSpeed Insights on mobile.

2. The first screen doesn't say where you work

“Quality roofing you can trust” could be anywhere. Fix: put your main service and towns in the headline: “Roof replacement and repair in Plano and North Dallas.”

3. There's no clear next step

Three buttons, a phone number in tiny text and a contact page buried in the menu. Fix: one main action — “Get a free inspection” — in the header, the hero, after each section, and in a sticky bar on phones.

4. The form is too long

Twelve fields, a CAPTCHA puzzle and no idea what happens next. Fix: ask only what you need to call back (name, phone, email, address or town, what they need) and say when you'll reply.

5. No proof

No reviews, no license number, no insurance, stock photos. Homeowners are deciding whether to trust you with their house. Fix: real Google reviews, license and insurance details, certifications you actually hold, warranty terms and real project photos — near the top. See what homeowners look for before hiring a roofer.

6. One page tries to do everything

Replacement, repair, gutters, siding and storm damage squeezed into one homepage. Google doesn't know what to rank it for, and homeowners can't find their answer. Fix: a page for each main service.

7. No town pages (or fake ones)

Either the site never mentions the towns you serve, or it has thirty identical pages with the city name swapped. Fix: a genuinely local page for each main town, with real projects and details.

8. Storm and insurance questions go unanswered

After hail or wind, homeowners want to know: is it damaged, will insurance pay, what's the process? Fix: a clear storm and insurance-help page explaining how you inspect and how claims usually work — without promising what insurance will pay.

9. It's built for the owner, not the homeowner

Long company history first, services last. Industry jargon. Fix: lead with the homeowner's problem and your answer; move the history to the About page.

10. Leads arrive, but nobody sees them

Form emails go to an old inbox or the spam folder, and the homeowner hires the roofer who called back first. Fix: send every lead to your phone instantly, test the form monthly, and save a copy of each lead somewhere safe.

If the problem is traffic

If your site converts the visitors it gets but simply doesn't get enough of them, the fixes are different:

  • Google Business Profile: complete it, pick the right category, add real photos and build steady reviews.
  • Titles and descriptions: each page needs a clear title with the service and town, like “Roof Replacement in Plano, TX.”
  • Service and town pages: give Google a specific page for each search you want to show up for.
  • Search Console: submit your sitemap and see which searches already show your site.
  • Google Ads: if you need visitors now, run tight campaigns while SEO builds.

A 15-minute self-test

  1. Open your site on your phone on mobile data. Count the seconds until you see the headline.
  2. Without scrolling: can you tell the service, the town and how to contact you?
  3. Find three pieces of proof (reviews, license, real photos) within one scroll.
  4. Fill in your own form. How many fields? Did the lead reach your phone within a minute?
  5. Search Google for “[your main service] [your town].” Which page of yours shows up, if any?

If you failed two or more, that's likely where your leads are leaking. Our roofing website checklist has 25 more checks.

What to fix first

Start with the fixes that cost little and touch every visitor: speed, the first screen, the next step and the form. Then add proof. Then service and town pages, which also help SEO. Quick answers

Why is my roofing website getting traffic but no calls?

Usually a mix of slow mobile loading, an unclear first screen, no obvious next step, too long a form and too little proof. Fix those before buying more traffic.

How many leads should a roofing website get?

It depends on your market, traffic and services. Instead of comparing to an average, track your own conversion rate — visits from your towns divided by leads — and improve it month by month.

Should I rebuild or fix my roofing website?

If it's on a slow, outdated platform or you can't edit it easily, a rebuild often costs less than patching. If it's reasonably modern, fixing the first screen, form and proof may be enough.

Can a new website hurt my Google rankings?

It can if old page addresses aren't redirected. A careful rebuild keeps your domain and redirects every old page to its new address.
