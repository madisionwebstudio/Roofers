How to market a roofing company, step by step.

Most roofing marketing advice is a list of 50 ideas. This is a plan: the few things that bring homeowners to your company, in the order to do them, with what each one costs in time and money.

Marketing a roofing company is different from marketing most local businesses. Homeowners buy a roof rarely, the job is expensive, and the decision often happens fast — after a storm, a leak or an insurance adjuster's visit. That means two things matter more than anything else: being visible at the moment a homeowner searches, and being trusted within the few seconds they spend comparing roofers.

Step 1: Fix the foundation first

Before spending a dollar on ads, get the basics that every other channel depends on right. Skipping this step is the most common reason roofing marketing “doesn't work”: the ads and rankings bring people in, and then something small sends them away.

  • One official business name, address and phone (NAP). Write it down once and use it identically everywhere — website, Google, Facebook, Yelp, the BBB, your truck wraps.
  • A clear list of services and towns. Which jobs do you want more of (replacement, repair, storm, commercial)? Which towns do you want them in? Marketing works better when it's specific.
  • Proof you can show. License number, insurance, manufacturer certifications you actually hold, warranty terms, and real photos of your crew and finished roofs.
  • A way to answer fast. Decide who answers calls and messages, and how quickly. Many homeowners contact more than one roofer, and the first to reply has a real edge.

None of this costs money. It costs an afternoon, and it makes every later step work better.

Step 2: Google Business Profile and reviews

For most “roofer near me” searches, Google shows a map with three businesses above the regular results. That map pack is driven largely by your Google Business Profile. Google says local rankings depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence [SOURCE NEEDED: link Google's “How Google determines local ranking” help page].

What to do:

  • Claim and verify your profile, set “Roofing contractor” as the primary category, and add only secondary categories that match work you really do.
  • List each service, set your service areas, add real photos and keep your hours current.
  • Never add keywords to your business name or use a fake address. Both are against Google's guidelines and a common reason roofing profiles get suspended.
  • Start a review routine: ask every happy customer, the day the job is done, with a direct link. Reply to every review — good and bad — briefly and professionally.

This is the highest-return thing most roofers can do in their first month. It's free, and you can do it yourself. Our guide on how to rank a roofing company on Google Maps goes deeper.

Step 3: A website that turns visits into calls

Your website is where Google, ads and word-of-mouth all send people. Homeowners use it to answer one question: “Can I trust this company with my house?” A good roofing website answers that in the first screen:

  • Who you are and which towns you serve, in plain words.
  • One clear next step — a short quote form, a message button — repeated down the page.
  • Real reviews, real project photos, license and insurance details.
  • A page for each main service: replacement, repair, storm and insurance help.
  • Fast on a phone. Many homeowners will look at your site on mobile, often outside, looking at their roof.

If your current site is slow, outdated or hard to use on a phone, fix it before you pay for traffic. Sending ad clicks to a weak site is the most expensive way to learn it's weak. Use our roofing website checklist to score your site, or read why roofing websites don't generate leads.

Step 4: SEO for steady leads

Roofing SEO is the work that gets your website and profile to show up when homeowners search — without paying per click. It's slower than ads, but the results build on each other: pages you write this year can keep bringing calls next year.

The core of SEO for a roofing company:

  • Service pages for each job you want more of, written for homeowners, not stuffed with keywords.
  • City pages for the towns you serve — only where you have genuine local detail to add.
  • Citations: your business details identical on the key directories.
  • Technical basics: fast pages, one H1 per page, clear titles and descriptions, a sitemap, Search Console set up.

Realistic timing: early movement in 1–3 months, stronger results in 3–6, longer in competitive metros. Anyone who guarantees a #1 ranking is guessing. More in our roofing SEO guide and on our roofing SEO services page.

Step 5: Google Ads for leads this week

Google Ads put you at the top of the results for the searches you choose, in the towns you choose, starting within days. For roofers, that usually means separate campaigns for replacement, repair and storm damage, with negative keywords to block job-seekers, DIY searches and towns you don't serve.

Step 6: Track every lead to its source

The difference between marketing that grows a roofing company and marketing that just costs money is knowing which channel brings signed jobs. Set this up before you spend on ads:

  • Google Analytics and Google Search Console on the website.
  • Form submissions, message clicks and calls counted as conversions.
  • Each lead saved with its source: Google Ads campaign, Maps, organic search, referral.
  • A simple sheet: lead → estimate → signed job → job value, by source, every month.

After two or three months, you'll know your real cost per signed job for each channel — and can move budget to whatever works best in your market.

Five mistakes that waste roofing marketing money

  • Paying for traffic before fixing the website. If the site is slow or confusing, every ad click costs more than it should.
  • Judging channels by lead count. Ten cheap leads that never sign are worse than three that do. Track signed jobs.
  • Trying every channel at once. Spreading a small budget across ads, social, mailers and directories usually means none gets enough to work.
  • Letting an agency own your accounts. Your domain, Google Business Profile and ad account should always be in your name.
  • Stopping too early. SEO needs months; ads need a few weeks of data. Decide your test period upfront and stick to it.

The order, on one page

  1. Week 1: business details, services and towns written down; proof collected; who answers leads decided.
  2. Weeks 1–2: Google Business Profile completed; review routine started.
  3. Weeks 2–4: website fixed or rebuilt, tracking installed.
  4. Month 2: Google Ads launched in your best towns if you need leads now.
  5. Months 2–6+: SEO — service pages, city pages, citations, reviews — building steady leads.
  6. Every month: check cost per signed job by channel and adjust.

You don't need every channel at once. A small roofing company with a strong profile, steady reviews and a fast, clear website can win a lot of local work before spending anything on ads. Quick answers

How much should a roofing company spend on marketing?

There is no single right number. Work backwards from the jobs you want: how many leads you need, what a lead costs in your best channel, and what a signed job is worth. Our guide on roofing marketing cost walks through the math.

What is the fastest way to get roofing customers?

Google search ads are usually the fastest high-intent channel, often bringing leads within the first one to two weeks — as long as your website is ready to convert the clicks.

Do I need a marketing agency?

No. Many roofers handle their Google profile, reviews and photos themselves. An agency is worth it when your time is better spent on jobs, or for technical work like website builds, SEO and ad management.

Is social media worth it for roofers?

It helps people remember you and is great for showing real jobs, but most homeowners who need a roof right now search Google. Treat social as support, not your main lead source.
