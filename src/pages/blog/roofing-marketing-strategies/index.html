Free strategies (time only)

1. Complete your Google Business Profile

The profile that appears in Google Maps is often the first thing a homeowner sees. Fill in every field: primary category “Roofing contractor,” services, service areas, hours, real photos, and a short description in plain words. Keep your business name exactly as it is in real life — no added keywords.

Cost: free. When: first, before anything else.

2. Build a steady review routine

Reviews affect both your Maps ranking and whether homeowners pick you. The routine that works: ask every customer on the day the job is finished, send a direct review link by text, and reply to every review within a few days. Never buy reviews or offer rewards for them — it breaks Google's rules and homeowners can often tell.

Cost: free. When: start now, keep going forever.

3. Post job photos every week

Before-and-after photos of real roofs are the most convincing marketing a roofer has. Add them to your Google profile, your website's project gallery and social media. Note the town and the material — homeowners want to see work near them.

Cost: free (a phone and five minutes per job).

4. Make your business details identical everywhere

Your name, address and phone should match exactly on your website, Google, Bing, Apple Maps, Facebook, Yelp, the BBB and the main directories. Mismatches can confuse search engines and homeowners alike.

Cost: free, or a small fee for a listing tool.

Website strategies

5. One page per service

A homeowner searching “roof leak repair” should land on a page about leak repair — not your homepage. Separate pages for replacement, repair, storm damage and insurance help, gutters or commercial work match what people search and give each job a clear next step.

Cost: your time to write, or part of a website project.

6. Real city pages for your main towns

City pages help you show up for searches like “roofer in [town].” They only work if they're genuinely local: jobs you did there, local weather and roof types, permit notes, reviews from that area. Three real pages beat thirty copy-paste ones.

7. A storm response page

After hail or high wind, searches for roof inspections and storm damage often jump. A ready-made storm page — what to do after a storm, how inspections work, how you help with insurance claims — lets you turn on ads quickly and answer the questions homeowners actually have.

8. Make contacting you effortless

A short form (name, phone, address, what they need), a message button, and the same call to action repeated down every page. Tell people what happens next and when you'll reply. Small changes here often matter more than more traffic.

Paid strategies

9. Google Ads for high-intent searches

Search ads let you appear for “roof replacement [town]” or “emergency roof repair” within days. Run separate campaigns for replacement, repair and storm, limit them to towns you serve, and add negative keywords for jobs, DIY and materials-only searches. Start with a budget you can sustain for at least 2–3 months so the data means something. See our Google Ads guide for roofing companies.

10. Local Services Ads (if you qualify)

Google's Local Services Ads appear above regular ads with a “Google Screened” or similar badge in many areas, and you pay per lead rather than per click. They're worth testing alongside search ads if roofing is offered in your market. LSA setup is included with our Google Ads management on request.

Offline and referral strategies

11. Turn every job into the next one

Yard signs (where allowed), door hangers for neighbors during the job, and a polite note asking for referrals all work because homeowners trust what they see on their street. Point every printed piece to one simple web page or message link so you can count the results.

12. Partner with people who see roofs first

Real estate agents, home inspectors, property managers and other trades (gutters, siding, solar) often know about roof problems before the homeowner calls anyone. A short, honest relationship — fast reports, reliable work — can bring steady referrals. Check your state's rules before paying for referrals.

Strategies to skip

  • Buying shared leads as your only channel. You race other roofers to the same homeowner, and nothing stays with your business when you stop. See roofing leads without Angi or HomeAdvisor.
  • Fake reviews, keyword-stuffed business names, fake addresses. These can get your Google profile suspended.
  • Dozens of thin city pages. Copy-paste pages rarely rank and can make your whole site look low quality.
  • Boosting random social posts. Fine for brand awareness, rarely the best first dollar for leads.

How to pick your first three

If you're starting from scratch, pick these three: (1) a complete Google Business Profile, (2) a review routine, and (3) a fast website with clear service pages. Add Google Ads when you need leads quickly and your website is ready, and SEO when you want leads that keep coming without paying per click.

Quick answers

What is the most effective roofing marketing strategy?

For most roofers, a complete Google Business Profile with steady reviews. It is free, it reaches homeowners who are actively searching, and every review keeps working.

How do roofers get more leads after a storm?

Have a storm page ready before the season, turn on storm-focused ad campaigns in affected towns, post updates on your Google profile, and answer every lead fast. Follow local rules on door-to-door sales.

Do door hangers still work for roofers?

They can, especially near active jobs where neighbors can see your crew. Point them to one simple web page or message link so you can count the results.

How long do roofing marketing strategies take to work?

Ads can work in days, Google profile fixes in weeks, and SEO in months. Plan to test any paid strategy for at least two to three months before judging it.
