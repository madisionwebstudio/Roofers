Rented leads vs. owned leads

Rented leads come from lead marketplaces and pay-per-lead services. You pay for each homeowner's request, and some services send the same request to several contractors. They're fast to start, but the homeowner remembers the marketplace, not you — and when you stop paying, the leads stop.

Owned leads come from homeowners finding your company: your Google profile, your website, your ads, your referrals. They take more setup, but every review, page and ranking you build stays with your business and makes the next lead cheaper.

Most roofers do best with a mix while their own system grows, then shift budget once they can compare the real cost per signed job.

Nine ways to get roofing leads

1. Google Maps (Google Business Profile)

For many local searches, the Maps pack is where homeowners click first. A complete profile with real photos, the right category, steady reviews and accurate service areas is the single best free lead source most roofers have. See how to rank a roofing company on Google Maps.

2. Reviews

Reviews don't just help rankings — they decide whether a homeowner calls you or the roofer next to you. Ask every customer, make it easy with a direct link, and reply to every review.

3. A website built to convert

Traffic is useless if the website loses it. The pages that convert best for roofers usually show the service area, real reviews and photos, licensing and insurance, financing or insurance-claim help, and one clear next step repeated down the page. A slow or confusing mobile site is one of the most common lead leaks we see.

4. Roofing SEO

Service pages, city pages, citations and technical fixes help you appear in regular search results for jobs and towns you care about. It's slower than ads — think months, not days — but the leads keep coming without a cost per click. Our roofing SEO services page explains what's involved.

5. Google Ads

The fastest way to get roofing leads from Google: you choose the searches, towns and budget, and ads can start bringing calls within days. Keep campaigns tight (replacement, repair, storm), block irrelevant searches, and send each ad to a matching page. See Google Ads for roofers.

6. Storm response

After hail or high wind, demand for inspections can spike in specific neighborhoods. Have a storm page ready, turn on storm ad campaigns in affected towns, and post updates on your Google profile. Always follow local rules on door-knocking and solicitation, and never pressure homeowners on insurance claims.

7. Referrals and neighbors

Every job is marketing to the street. Yard signs where allowed, a friendly note to neighbors, and asking happy customers to pass your name on still bring some of the best-quality leads.

8. Partners

Real estate agents, home inspectors, insurance agents, property managers and other trades see roof problems early. Be the roofer who sends clear reports quickly and shows up when you say you will.

9. Lead marketplaces (carefully)

Services like Angi and HomeAdvisor can fill gaps, especially when you're new. Track them like any other channel — cost per signed job, not cost per lead — and decide with real numbers. More in how to get roofing leads without Angi or HomeAdvisor.

How to get better-quality roofing leads

More leads isn't always the goal. Most roofers would rather have fewer leads that turn into replacements than a flood of tire-kickers. A few things help:

  • Say what you do — and don't. If you don't do small repairs or work outside certain towns, say so on the website and in your ads.
  • Target the jobs you want. Separate ad campaigns and pages for replacement, repair and storm let you put more budget where the profit is.
  • Block bad searches. Negative keywords stop ads showing for jobs, DIY, materials and towns you don't serve.
  • Ask one qualifying question. “What do you need help with?” on the form lets you prioritize callbacks without scaring people off.
  • Show price ranges or financing where you can, so homeowners with a very different budget self-select out.

Speed to lead: the part most roofers miss

Homeowners often contact more than one roofer. Whatever the exact numbers in your market, the lesson is simple:

  • Make sure every web lead reaches your phone instantly — not a shared inbox checked once a day.
  • Tell the homeowner when you'll reply (on the thank-you page and in an auto email).
  • Decide who answers during jobs, evenings and weekends.
  • Follow up at least twice if you can't reach them the first time.

How to know which leads are worth it

Count leads, but judge channels by signed jobs and revenue. A simple monthly sheet does the job:

  • Lead date, name, source (Maps, organic, Google Ads campaign, referral, marketplace).
  • Estimate given? Job signed? Job value?
  • Total spend per source that month.

Divide spend by signed jobs for each source. After a few months the answer to “where should my next dollar go?” is usually obvious. Make sure your website forms save the source automatically — UTM tags and the Google click ID for ads — so you're not guessing.

A simple 90-day plan

  1. Days 1–14: complete Google Business Profile, start the review routine, collect real photos, make sure leads reach your phone instantly.
  2. Days 15–45: fix or rebuild the website, add tracking, write your main service pages.
  3. Days 30–60: launch Google Ads in your best towns if you need leads now.
  4. Days 45–90: start SEO — city pages, citations, more reviews — and compare cost per signed job by channel.

Quick answers

What is the best way to get roofing leads?

For most roofers: a complete Google Business Profile with steady reviews, a website that makes contacting you easy, and Google Ads when you need leads quickly. Add SEO for leads that keep coming.

Are bought roofing leads worth it?

They can fill gaps, especially for new companies. Judge them by cost per signed job, not cost per lead, and remember that some leads may also go to other contractors.

How quickly should I respond to a roofing lead?

As fast as you can — ideally within minutes during business hours. Homeowners often contact more than one roofer, and the first to reply has a real advantage.

Can I get roofing leads without paying for ads?

Yes. A strong Google profile, reviews, referrals and SEO can bring leads without paying per click. They take longer to build than ads.
