How to get roofing leads without Angi or HomeAdvisor

Shared leads, rising bills and nothing to show when you stop paying. Here is how to build lead sources your roofing company actually owns.

Lead marketplaces like Angi and HomeAdvisor can fill a schedule when you're starting out. But many roofers end up frustrated: the same homeowner sent to several contractors, leads that never answer the phone, and a monthly bill that keeps growing while nothing is being built for the business.

This guide is about the alternative — building your own lead sources that you control. We'll be fair about where lead marketplaces fit, and specific about what to build instead. Why roofers want off shared leads

  • Shared leads. A homeowner request can go to more than one contractor, so you're racing others to the phone and competing on price from the first call.
  • You're renting, not owning. When you stop paying, the leads stop — and nothing you paid for stays with your business.
  • Your brand stays invisible. The homeowner remembers the marketplace, not you. Next time, they go back to the marketplace.
  • Less control. You choose less about which jobs, towns and homeowners you get.

That doesn't make marketplaces useless. If you're brand new with no reviews and no website, they can bring early jobs while you build. The goal is to depend on them less every month.

The 5 lead sources you can own

1. A website that turns visits into calls

Everything else sends people to your website, so it has to earn their trust and make calling easy. That means real photos, Google reviews, licensing, a clear service area, storm and insurance help, financing, a short quote form and a tap-to-call number on every screen. We cover the full list in what homeowners look for before hiring a roofer.

A website you own is an asset. A lead you buy is a one-time event.

2. Your Google Business Profile

For local searches, your Google Business Profile is often seen before your website. A complete profile — right category, services, service area, real photos and steady reviews — puts you in the Maps results when homeowners search for a roofer nearby. It's free, and it compounds. See how to rank in the Google Maps top 3.

3. SEO for your services and towns

Pages for roof replacement, roof repair, storm damage and your key towns let you rank in the regular results below the map. SEO takes a few months to build — early movement in 1–3 months, stronger results in 3–6 — but then keeps bringing calls without paying per lead or per click. Read why your roofing website needs SEO.

4. Google Ads (and Local Services Ads)

Google Ads gives you leads now, but unlike a marketplace, the homeowner is calling you — not choosing between five roofers on a list. You control the towns, the job types and the budget, and the account and its history are yours. Local Services Ads charge per lead, but the lead is yours alone. See Google Ads for roofers.

5. Referrals and reviews, done on purpose

Referrals are a roofer's best leads, but most companies leave them to chance. A simple routine changes that: ask for a Google review the day the job is finished, send a thank-you with your card, and check in after the next big storm. Each review makes your profile, your website and your ads convert better.

Turning happy customers into your next leads

The cheapest lead you'll ever get is the neighbor who watched your crew work. Make it easy for them: keep the job site tidy, put a yard sign up with permission, and leave a card with the homeowner. When you ask for a Google review, ask the customer to mention the work and their town — reviews like that help the next homeowner in that town choose you, and they help your Maps ranking too.

A 90-day plan to depend less on lead marketplaces

  1. Weeks 1–2: fix your website basics — phone number one tap away, real photos, reviews, quote form. Or get a new one.
  2. Weeks 2–3: complete your Google Business Profile; start asking every finished customer for a review.
  3. Week 3: start Google Ads on your most profitable job type in your best towns, with call tracking.
  4. Month 2: add service pages and your first real city page; keep the review routine going.
  5. Month 3: compare cost per job across marketplace, Google Ads and organic. Shift budget toward what works.

Track cost per job, not cost per lead

A cheap lead that never answers is expensive. Track each source by the jobs it produces: how many leads, how many inspections, how many signed contracts, and the revenue. Put a tracking number on your Google profile and ads, and note the source of every lead in one place. After three months the right budget split is usually obvious.

How the pieces work together

None of these sources works as well alone as they do together. Picture one homeowner after a hailstorm:

  1. They search “hail damage roof inspection” plus their town. Your Google Ad shows at the top, and your Google Business Profile shows in the map below it with 60 recent reviews.
  2. They tap the map listing first, read three reviews mentioning their neighborhood, then tap through to your website.
  3. Your storm page explains the free inspection and the insurance process, shows photos of jobs on their street, and has a call button at the bottom of the screen.
  4. They call. You answer. You book the inspection.
  5. After the job, you ask for a review — which helps the next homeowner choose you.

Every step makes the others stronger. That's the difference from a marketplace lead, where the homeowner never gets to know your company at all.

How to split a monthly marketing budget

There's no single right split, but a common starting point for a roofer moving off marketplaces looks like this: first, a website that converts (a one-time or monthly cost); second, Google Ads with enough budget to learn — at least $2,000/month in ad spend; third, SEO for your main town; and an ongoing, free review routine. Review cost per job every month and move money toward whatever is producing signed contracts.

Speed to lead: the part nobody talks about

Owning your lead sources only pays off if you answer them. Homeowners who request a roofing quote often contact more than one company, and the first roofer to respond has a real advantage [SOURCE NEEDED: study on how quickly contractors reply to web leads]. A few habits make a big difference:

  • Answer calls live during working hours, or use an answering service — a missed call is often a lost job.
  • Reply to web forms within the hour when you can, and set an honest expectation on the thank-you page (“we'll call you today”).
  • Get form leads on your phone the moment they arrive, so nothing sits in an inbox overnight.
  • Follow up twice if you can't reach someone — a call and a text — then stop.

Before you cancel a lead marketplace

Don't switch everything off at once. Ask yourself:

  • Do I know my cost per signed job from the marketplace, not just cost per lead?
  • Is my website ready to convert traffic I send it?
  • Is my Google Business Profile complete, with recent reviews?
  • Do I have call tracking so I can compare sources fairly?
  • Do I have a replacement source running — usually Google Ads — before I cut the old one?

If the answer to all five is yes, reduce marketplace spend step by step while watching jobs per month.

Quick answers

Are Angi or HomeAdvisor leads always bad?

No. Some roofers do well with them, especially early on. The risk is depending on a source you don't control. Use them as one channel, not the whole plan.

What's the cheapest way to get roofing leads?

Over time, the leads you don't pay for per click or per lead: referrals, reviews, your Google Business Profile and SEO. They take effort and time, but they compound.

How fast can I replace marketplace leads?

Google Ads can bring calls within weeks. SEO and Maps take months. That's why most roofers run ads while they build the long-term sources.
