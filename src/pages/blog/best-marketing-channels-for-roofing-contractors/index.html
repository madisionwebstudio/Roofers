The best marketing channels for roofing contractors.

Every channel can work for some roofer somewhere. The question is which ones fit your budget, your timeline and the jobs you want. Here's an honest comparison.

Intent matters most in roofing. Someone searching “roof leak repair near me” has a problem today. Someone scrolling Facebook may need a roof in two years — or never.

Google channels

Google Business Profile (Maps)

The best channel for most roofers, especially smaller ones. It's free, high-intent, and every review and photo you add keeps working. The catch: distance from the searcher is part of the ranking, so it works best in and near the town where you're based. Read local SEO for roofing companies.

SEO

Website rankings reach homeowners researching specific jobs (“metal roof cost,” “hail damage roof inspection”) and searches in towns slightly farther from your office. Slow to build, but compounding. See our roofing SEO guide.

Social media

Facebook and Instagram are good for staying visible — project photos, crew stories, storm updates — and for neighborhood word-of-mouth. As a lead channel, paid social usually reaches people who aren't actively looking for a roofer, so expect lower intent and more follow-up. It can work well for specific offers (free inspections after a storm, financing) and retargeting people who already visited your site. We don't run social ads.

Referrals and offline

Referrals from past customers, real estate agents, inspectors and other trades often produce the best-quality leads because trust is already there. Yard signs and door hangers near active jobs work because neighbors can see your work. Make it easy to track: a short web address or QR code that leads to one page.

Lead marketplaces

Pay-per-lead services are quick to start and useful for filling gaps. The trade-offs: leads may go to several contractors, you compete on speed and price, and nothing stays with your business. Track cost per signed job, not per lead. More in roofing leads without Angi or HomeAdvisor.

Email and past customers

Your past customers are an easy channel to overlook. A short, useful email or text a couple of times a year — storm season reminders, a gutter check offer, a request for referrals — keeps you in mind when a neighbor asks “who did your roof?” Only contact people who agreed to hear from you, and make unsubscribing easy.

Channels change with the season

Roofing demand isn't flat. Storm season, winter slowdowns and spring replacement demand all change which channel deserves the most attention:

  • Storm season: storm-specific ad campaigns, a storm page, frequent Google profile posts and fast response matter most.
  • Busy season: you may need fewer paid leads — keep reviews and SEO going so slower months are fuller.
  • Slow season: a good time to build pages, collect photos, update your profile and test new ad campaigns while competition may be lower [SOURCE NEEDED: seasonal CPC data for your market].

Whatever the season, keep the always-on basics running: reviews, photos and a fast website.

How to score a channel for your company

Before adding any channel, answer four questions:

  1. Are the people it reaches actively looking for a roofer? Higher intent usually means a lower cost per signed job.
  2. Can I track leads from it? If you can't measure it, you can't compare it.
  3. What stays with my business if I stop? Reviews, rankings and content keep working; rented leads don't.
  4. Can I give it enough budget and time? A channel tested for three weeks on a tiny budget tells you very little.

The mix we'd start with

  1. Always: a complete Google Business Profile and a review routine.
  2. Always: a fast website that makes contacting you easy, with tracking.
  3. If you need leads now: Google search ads in your best towns.
  4. For steady leads later: SEO — service pages, city pages, citations.
  5. On the side: referrals, yard signs, social posts of real jobs.

Quick answers

What is the best marketing channel for roofers?

For most roofing contractors it is Google — the Maps listing first, then website rankings and search ads — because homeowners searching Google usually need a roofer now.

Should roofers advertise on Facebook?

It can work for brand awareness, storm offers and retargeting site visitors. For leads from homeowners who need a roof now, Google usually performs better.

Are Local Services Ads good for roofers?

Where they are available for roofing, many contractors find them worth testing because you pay per lead. Availability and rules vary by area.

How many marketing channels should a roofer use?

Start with two or three you can do well — usually Google profile, website and either ads or SEO — then add more once you can measure results.
