What “SEO” means for a roofer

Search engine optimization is the work of getting your business to show up — without paying per click — when homeowners search Google for things like “roof repair near me” or “roofer in [your town].”

For a local trade like roofing, there are two places you want to appear:

  • The map results (often called the “map pack” or “local pack”): the map with a short list of businesses near the top of the page. This is driven mostly by your Google Business Profile, your reviews and how close you are to the person searching. Google explains that local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence [SOURCE NEEDED: link to Google Business Profile Help — how local ranking works].
  • The regular results below the map: the blue links. These are driven mostly by your website — its pages, its content, how fast it is, and how many other trustworthy sites link to it.

Above both, you'll often see paid ads: Google Ads and Local Services Ads. Those aren't SEO; you pay for them. We cover them separately on our Google Ads page.

When SEO is worth it

SEO tends to be a good investment for a roofing company when most of these are true:

  1. You want to grow beyond referrals. Referrals are great, but they're hard to scale. Search is where homeowners without a referral go.
  2. You can handle more work. If your crews are booked for months, more leads won't help — fix capacity first.
  3. You plan to be in the same area for years. SEO builds value over time in specific towns. It's a long-term asset, not a quick fix.
  4. You have, or are getting, a solid website. SEO sends people to your site. If the site is slow or confusing, you pay to send visitors somewhere they won't call from.
  5. You can collect reviews. Reviews matter a lot for map rankings and for whether people call. You'll need a simple habit of asking happy customers for them.

The big advantage over ads is that SEO keeps working when you stop spending on it for a while. A page that ranks keeps bringing calls month after month, and a strong Google Business Profile keeps showing up in the map.

When it isn't (yet)

SEO is probably not your best next dollar if:

  • You need calls this month. SEO takes months. If cash flow is tight, Google Ads or Local Services Ads can get the phone ringing faster — and you can add SEO later.
  • Your website is the problem. If the site is slow, broken on phones or looks untrustworthy, fix that first. Otherwise, more traffic just means more people leaving.
  • You haven't claimed your Google Business Profile. Do that first (it's free) and work through the do-it-yourself list below. You might get a meaningful bump for zero dollars.
  • You're about to move or rebrand. Changing your business name, address or phone number mid-campaign creates messy, inconsistent listings. Settle those first.

How long it takes

For most roofing companies, real momentum from SEO shows up in roughly 3 to 6 months. That's a typical range, not a promise — it can be faster in small towns with little competition and much slower in big metros where dozens of roofers have been doing SEO for years.

What affects the timeline:

  • Competition. How many roofers in your area are already doing this well.
  • Your starting point. An established profile with lots of reviews moves faster than a new one.
  • Reviews. A steady stream of genuine reviews helps both rankings and calls.
  • How many towns you target. One city is faster than five.

Be wary of anyone who promises rankings by a specific date, or guarantees the number-one spot. Google says plainly that no one can guarantee a top ranking [SOURCE NEEDED: Google Search Central — “Do you need an SEO?” guidance]. Those guarantees usually mean ranking you for phrases nobody searches.

What you can do yourself this week

Before paying anyone, do these. They're free and they matter.

  1. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile. Correct business name (no keyword stuffing — it can get you suspended), the right primary category (“Roofing contractor”), your services, service areas, hours and phone.
  2. Add real photos. Your trucks, crew and finished jobs. Add new ones regularly.
  3. Ask for reviews. After every finished job, send the customer a direct link to leave a Google review. Reply to every review, good or bad.
  4. Make your name, address and phone identical everywhere. Your website, Google, Facebook, Yelp, the BBB and any directories. Small differences confuse search engines.
  5. Give each service its own page. Replacement, repair, storm damage. One page per job type.
  6. Check your site on your phone. If it's slow or hard to use, that's the first thing to fix. Our 25-point checklist will help.

How to hire someone without getting burned

If you decide to pay for SEO, these questions separate the good providers from the rest:

  • What exactly will you do each month? You should get a specific list — pages written, profile updates, citations, links — not “optimization.”
  • How will you report results? Look for calls and leads by source, not just rankings for phrases you've never heard of.
  • Who owns the work if we stop? Your profile, your pages, your data. All of it should stay with you.
  • How long is the contract? Month to month is ideal. Long lock-ins with no exit are a red flag.
  • Do you write reviews or buy links? The right answer is “never.” Fake reviews can get your profile penalized and break the FTC's rules [SOURCE NEEDED: FTC rule on fake reviews].
  • Do you work with other roofers in my area? Some agencies do. Ask how they handle it.

Signs your SEO is working

Rankings are only part of the picture, and they bounce around day to day. These are better signals, roughly in the order you'll see them:

  • More impressions in Google Search Console — you're showing up for more searches, even before people click.
  • More profile actions — calls, website clicks and direction requests from your Google Business Profile.
  • More calls and form leads from organic and map traffic, tracked by source.
  • Better leads — homeowners asking about replacements in the towns you targeted, not random repair calls two counties away.

If after six months none of these have moved, ask hard questions — of us or of anyone else.
