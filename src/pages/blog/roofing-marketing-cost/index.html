What you're actually paying for

“Roofing marketing” is usually a bundle of separate things. Pricing gets confusing when they're mixed into one monthly fee, so separate them:

  • Website — design, build, hosting, updates.
  • SEO — Google Business Profile, reviews routine, citations, pages, technical fixes, reporting.
  • Paid ads — the ad spend paid to Google (or Meta), plus management.
  • Leads you buy — pay-per-lead marketplaces.
  • Tools — call tracking, CRM, review software, scheduling.
  • Your time — answering leads, photos, approving content.

Hidden costs to ask about

  • Who owns the domain, website, Google profile and ad account? If the agency owns them, leaving can mean starting over.
  • Long contracts. 12–24 month lock-ins with no performance terms.
  • Setup fees that aren't in the monthly price.
  • Ad spend mixed into the management fee, so you can't see what Google actually received.
  • Extra charges for content, photos, landing pages, or “premium” reporting.
  • Tools you'll need anyway — call tracking, CRM, review software.

What to spend at each stage of your business

New or small roofing company

Keep cash costs low and put in time instead: Google Business Profile, a review routine, real photos and a simple, fast website. If you need leads quickly, a modest Google Ads test in your home town — once the site is ready — will show you real numbers.

Established company wanting steady growth

A stronger website with a page per service and main town, monthly SEO, and Google Ads for the jobs with the best margin. Track cost per signed job by channel and review it monthly.

Company expanding to new towns

Ads give you visibility in a new area right away while you build real local pages and reviews there. Expect Maps rankings in towns far from your address to be harder — distance is part of how Google ranks local results.

How to set your budget

Work backwards from the jobs you want, not from a percentage you read online:

  1. How many extra jobs a month do you want? Say, four replacements.
  2. What's your close rate on estimates? If you sign one in three, you need about 12 estimates.
  3. How many leads become estimates? If half do, you need about 24 leads.
  4. What does a lead cost in your best channel? Multiply. That's your monthly marketing budget for that goal.

Then compare that number with the profit from four replacements. If the math works, spend; if not, fix conversion (website, speed to lead, close rate) before adding budget. Use your own numbers — every market is different.

Judge cost by signed jobs

The cheapest marketing isn't the one with the lowest invoice — it's the one with the lowest cost per signed job. A $50 lead that's shared with four other roofers can cost more per job than a $150 lead that only you received. Track every lead to its source and review it monthly.

Quick answers

What percentage of revenue should a roofer spend on marketing?

You'll see rules of thumb online, but they vary widely and rarely fit a specific company. Set your budget from the number of jobs you want and your real cost per signed job instead.

Is SEO cheaper than Google Ads for roofers?

Over time it often costs less per lead because you don't pay per click — but it takes months to build. Ads cost more per lead but work right away. Many roofers run both. See roofing SEO vs Google Ads.

Should ad spend be included in the agency fee?

It's clearer when it isn't. You should pay Google directly from your own ad account, so you can always see exactly what was spent.

Do I need to sign a long contract?

Not necessarily. Our SEO is month to month. The Pro Site monthly plan has a 12-month minimum because it spreads the build cost; the one-time option has no contract.

What is the cheapest way to market a roofing company?

Your Google Business Profile, reviews, real job photos and referrals cost little more than your time — and they make every paid channel work better later.
