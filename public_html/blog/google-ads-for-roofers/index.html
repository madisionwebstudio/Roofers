Google Ads for roofing companies: the complete guide

The fastest way to get roofing leads — and the fastest way to waste money. Here is how to budget, set up and judge Google Ads for a roofing company.

Google Ads is the fastest way for a roofing company to get in front of homeowners who are searching right now. It's also the fastest way to spend a lot of money on clicks that never call. The difference is almost entirely in the setup, the budget and what happens after the click.

This guide covers how much to budget, what drives cost per lead, how to set up campaigns that work for roofers, and what to expect in the first months. Where we'd need a market figure, we've marked it [SOURCE NEEDED] rather than guess — roofing click costs vary a lot by city and season.

How Google Ads works for roofers

You choose the searches you want to show up for (for example, “roof replacement” or “roof leak repair” in your service area), write ads, and set a budget. When a homeowner searches, Google runs an auction. You pay when someone clicks your ad — not when it's shown.

Google Ads sits alongside two other things homeowners see on the results page:

  • Local Services Ads (LSA) — the ads with a Google badge at the very top. You pay per lead instead of per click, and Google has to verify your business first.
  • The Maps pack and organic results — earned through SEO, not paid per click.

Many roofers run Google Ads for leads now while SEO builds for the long term.

How much budget do you need?

Roofing is one of the more expensive local categories to advertise in, because a single replacement job is worth thousands [SOURCE NEEDED: Google Ads keyword planner CPC range for roofing terms in your market]. That has a practical consequence: a campaign needs enough clicks every month to learn which searches, towns and ads turn into jobs.

That's why we set a minimum ad budget of $2,000/month, paid directly to Google. Below that, there usually aren't enough clicks for the data to mean anything, and results come down to luck.

Your exact budget should come from your market: check estimated cost per click for your main searches in Google's Keyword Planner, then ask how many clicks you need to get a handful of real leads.

What drives cost per lead

Cost per lead = what you spend ÷ the calls and forms you get. Five things move it more than anything else:

1. Targeting

Show ads only in towns and ZIP codes you actually serve, and only to people in (or searching for) those places. Wide targeting wastes money on homeowners you'd never drive to.

2. Keywords and negative keywords

Bid on searches with real buying intent — “roof replacement,” “roof repair near me,” “hail damage roof inspection.” Then block the searches that waste money: “roofing jobs,” “roofing salary,” “DIY roof repair,” “shingle prices,” “roofing supplies.” Reviewing the search terms report every week is where most savings come from.

3. Separate campaigns by job type

Split replacement, repair and storm damage into their own campaigns. Each has a different value, a different homeowner mindset and a different landing page. It also lets you push budget toward the work you want most.

4. The landing page

The click is only half the job. If the page is slow, generic or hides your phone number, you pay for visitors who leave. A good roofing landing page matches the ad (a storm ad lands on your storm page), loads fast on a phone, shows reviews and licensing, and has one clear action: call or request a quote.

5. Conversion tracking

Without call and form tracking, you can't tell which keywords bring jobs, and Google can't optimize for them. Track calls from ads, calls from your website and form submissions as separate conversions.

How to set up roofing campaigns

  1. Account in your name. Create it yourself or have it created under your business, billed to your card. Never let an agency own your account.
  2. Conversion tracking first. Calls and forms, before any money is spent.
  3. Three core campaigns: roof replacement, roof repair, storm damage — each with its own keywords, ads and landing page.
  4. Location targeting set to the areas you serve.
  5. A starting negative keyword list (jobs, DIY, supplies, prices, materials).
  6. Ad copy for homeowners: local, specific, with a reason to call now (free inspection, financing, licensed and insured).
  7. Ad schedule matched to when you can answer the phone.
  8. Weekly review of search terms, budgets and bids.

Should you also run Local Services Ads?

Often, yes. LSA shows above regular ads with a Google badge, and you pay per lead rather than per click. The trade-offs: Google has to verify your business (licensing, insurance and background checks where required), you have less control over which searches trigger your ad, and you should dispute leads that aren't genuine. Many roofers run both.

What to expect in the first months

  • Week 1: setup — tracking, campaigns, keywords, ads, landing page check.
  • Weeks 2–4: ads live; first calls usually come within the first 1–2 weeks. Lots of search-term cleanup.
  • Month 2 onward: budget shifts toward the job types and towns that produce real jobs, and cost per lead usually improves.

Weather matters: demand jumps after storms and drops in some seasons. A good manager adjusts budgets with it.

A simple way to think about the numbers

Before spending anything, work backwards from a job. Use your own numbers — these are just to show the method, not market data:

  1. Average profit per job. What does a typical replacement or repair leave you after materials and labor?
  2. Close rate. Out of every ten real roofing leads you inspect, how many sign?
  3. Maximum cost per lead. Profit per job × close rate gives you what a lead is worth. Decide what share of that you're willing to spend to get one.
  4. Clicks needed. Divide your monthly budget by the estimated cost per click in your market to see how many clicks you'll get; your landing page decides how many become calls.

This turns “is Google Ads worth it?” from a feeling into a number you can check every month in your weekly report. If cost per lead is above what a lead is worth, something has to change: targeting, keywords, the landing page — or the budget.

Remember that not every lead is equal. A replacement lead is worth far more than a small repair, which is why separate campaigns by job type matter: you can afford to pay more for the leads that bring bigger jobs.

Common Google Ads mistakes roofers make

  • Sending every ad to the homepage. A storm ad should land on a storm page; a repair ad on a repair page. Message match lifts conversions.
  • Broad keywords with no negatives. “Roof” alone can match jobs, supplies, car roofs and DIY videos.
  • Targeting a whole metro you don't fully serve. Every click from a town you won't drive to is wasted.
  • No call tracking. Many roofing leads are phone calls. If calls aren't tracked, the campaign looks worse than it is — and Google optimizes for the wrong thing.
  • Turning ads off and on. Constant stopping and starting makes it harder for campaigns to learn. Adjust budgets instead.
  • Letting “smart” settings run unchecked. Automated bidding can work well, but only with good conversion data and a human reviewing search terms.
  • Ads running when nobody answers. Schedule ads for when someone can pick up, or make sure calls are answered.

A storm-season playbook

When hail or high winds hit your area, search demand for roof inspections can jump overnight. Have this ready before it happens:

  1. A storm damage campaign, built and paused, with ads about free inspections and insurance claim help.
  2. An up-to-date storm page on your website, with the affected areas and a short inspection form.
  3. Location targeting you can narrow to the neighborhoods that were hit.
  4. A budget increase agreed in advance, so it can go live the same morning.
  5. Someone answering the phone — speed to response wins storm jobs.

Quick answers

Is Google Ads worth it for a small roofing company?

Often yes, if you can handle more jobs, have a website that converts, and can commit to at least $2,000/month in ad spend for a few months so the campaign can learn.

How long before I know if it works?

Calls usually start in the first 1–2 weeks. Give it at least the first full month, with tracking, before judging cost per lead.

Should I run ads if I'm already doing SEO?

Many roofers do both: ads for leads now and in storm season, SEO for steady long-term leads. As organic calls grow, you can shift ad budget.
