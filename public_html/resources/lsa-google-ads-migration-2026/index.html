LSA is moving into Google Ads (2026): what roofers need to do

Roofing is in the first wave. Here's what changes, what you lose if you don't prepare, and a checklist to work through before your account moves.

Last updated: November 9, 20269 min readBy the Roofrs team

On this page

  1. What's changing
  2. When it happens
  3. LSA as a Performance Max campaign
  4. Download your LSA history now
  5. Blended targets across services

  7. Checklist before you migrate
  8. Sources

What's changing

Local Services Ads — the “Google Guaranteed” or “Google Screened” listings with a green check at the very top of the results — have always lived in their own dashboard, separate from Google Ads. That's ending. Google is moving LSA into Google Ads, and roofing is in the first wave of trades to move.

For roofers this is more than a new login. How LSA campaigns are set up, how budgets and bids are controlled, and how results are reported all change. If you run both LSA and regular search ads, they'll now sit in the same account and can compete with each other if nobody manages it.

Short version
  • LSA becomes a campaign type inside Google Ads.
  • Your old LSA reports don't carry over — download them first.
  • Missed calls can cost you more than before, so answer rate matters.

When it happens

The migration began in August 2026, with roofing among the first categories. Google is moving accounts in batches, and you'll usually get an email and a banner in your LSA dashboard before your account moves. Don't wait for it — the prep work below takes a couple of hours and can't be done after the fact.

LSA as a Performance Max campaign type

Inside Google Ads, Local Services Ads run as a Performance Max campaign type with pay-per-lead goals. In plain English: you still pay per lead rather than per click, but the campaign is managed with Google Ads tools — budgets, goals, conversion settings and reporting — instead of the simple LSA dashboard.

That brings more control, and more ways to get it wrong. Conversion settings, which lead types count, and how the campaign shares budget with your other campaigns all need a deliberate setup.

“If nobody owns the setup, LSA and your search campaigns end up bidding against each other for the same homeowner.”What we see in mixed LSA + search accounts

Download your LSA history now

Historical LSA reports don't carry over into Google Ads. Once your account moves, the lead history, charged/credited leads, call recordings and review data in the old dashboard may no longer be available in the same form.

Do this today
  1. Export your full lead report (all time, or as far back as it goes).
  2. Download call recordings for leads that turned into jobs — they're your best training material for the office.
  3. Screenshot your profile: categories, service areas, hours, budget, reviews and badge status.
  4. Note your average cost per lead by job type and month. You'll need it to judge whether the new setup is better or worse.

Blended targets if multiple services share an account

If you offer more than one service — roof replacement, repair, gutters, siding — and they share one account, the new setup can use blended targets across them. A cheap gutter lead and an expensive replacement lead get averaged together, and the system may chase the cheaper one.

Decide up front which job types you actually want, what each is worth to you, and whether they should be separated. A $250 lead is a bargain for a full replacement and a disaster for a gutter cleaning.

Why answer rate matters more now

With pay-per-lead campaigns, some missed calls can still be billable leads. That means a call you never answered can show up on your invoice. LSA has always rewarded responsiveness in ranking; now the cost of missing calls is more direct.

This is the same leak we write about everywhere else on this site: if your crew is on a roof and nobody picks up, you pay for the lead and the homeowner calls the next roofer. A missed-call text-back and a clear process for disputing invalid leads are no longer optional.

You can't dispute your way out of a low answer rate.Fix the phones before the migration

Checklist before your account moves

  • Export all LSA lead history, call recordings and profile details.
  • Write down your current cost per lead and cost per signed job by job type.
  • List the job types you want, and the ones you don't (turn those off).
  • Check that your Google Business Profile, license and insurance details are current — verification issues delay everything.
  • Set up missed-call text-back on the number LSA rings.
  • Make sure call tracking or call recording is on, so you can match leads to signed jobs.
  • Decide who owns the Google Ads account (it should be you) and who manages it.
  • If you run regular search ads too, plan how LSA and search budgets and keywords will divide up.
